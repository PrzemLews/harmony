The 16-step grid

A bar of dance music has four beats, and the kick usually lands on every one of them. Split each beat into four and you get the 16-step grid that drum machines and DAWs use: each step is a 1/16 note.

Count the four steps in every beat as positions 1, 2, 3, 4. Position 1 sits right on the kick. Position 3 is the off-beat, halfway between two kicks, where the open hi-hat usually goes. Positions 2 and 4 are the in-between 16ths.

Where your bass notes sit against the kick decides most of how a bassline feels. Pick a preset, press Play, then click squares to add or remove notes.

Note lengths and rhythm families

Note lengths are fractions of one bar. A 1/16 fills one step, a 1/8 two steps, a 1/4 one whole beat (four steps), and 1/1 the whole bar. A dotted note is half again as long: a dotted 1/8 lasts three steps.

Most electronic basslines start from one of five rhythm families. Picking the family is the first decision for each section of a track; switching family between sections (a dotted line in the breakdown, a straight one in the drop) is the cheapest way to add contrast.

Each family below loops over a simple four-chord progression so you hear it in a musical setting. Try the sidechain switch on the straight 1/8.

Groove

A bassline grooves when it avoids the obvious. Four habits do most of the work:

  • Move notes off the kick. Everything on position 1 sounds solid but flat.
  • Mix note lengths. Short and long notes in one loop sound played, not programmed.
  • Leave rests. Gaps give the bass rhythm and leave room for the percussion.
  • Nudge it off the grid. Swing delays every second 16th a little, which adds a human lilt.

    Notes that move

    Once the rhythm grooves, pitch makes the bass dance. The scale sets the mood, and a few intervals around the root do the moving:

    • 5th: the safest move away from the root. It never clashes.
    • ♭7: adds a bouncy, house-like swing.
    • ♭3: colour, and a more melodic line.
    • ♭2 (Phrygian): one half step above the root. This is where dark tech house and afro basslines get their tension.
    • 8, the octave: movement without changing the harmony.

    Rows are scale degrees, numbered from the root. Switch the scale while the loop plays and listen to the same pattern change mood. Play along on your keyboard: the row you hit lights up.

    Layers and sound

    A modern electronic bass is rarely one sound. It is a stack of layers, each with one job, acting as one instrument:

    • Sub: a near-pure sine below about 120 Hz. You feel it on a club system more than you hear it.
    • Mid-bass: a saw wave with its lows cut. This is the groove you hear on any speaker, phones included.
    • Fifth on top: an extra saw seven half steps up for bite, without new harmony.

    Three controls shape the mid layer. Pluck is a filter envelope: it opens bright on each note and closes fast. Drive adds distortion and harmonics. Resonance makes the filter squelch, the acid sound. Last, sidechain ducks the bass every time the kick hits, so the two don't fight and the bass pumps.
